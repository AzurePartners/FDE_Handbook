**In one sentence:** A postmortem is an honest written retrospective, what broke, why it broke, what changed, and how to prevent recurrence, done blamelessly and never turned into success marketing, so a finished engagement (or an incident) produces durable lessons.

## What it is

A **postmortem** is a structured reflection after a project phase or incident. It covers: **what broke** (or what was set out vs delivered), **why** (root causes, not surface symptoms), **what changed** as a result, and **how to prevent recurrence** (concrete actions). Its defining qualities: it's **blameless**, focused on systems, decisions, and process rather than individual fault, which is what makes people honest enough to surface real causes; and it's **not success marketing**, a postmortem that only lists wins is useless, the value is in the honest accounting of what went wrong and what to change.

## Why an FDE needs this

FDEs hit similar problems across engagements; a postmortem is how the same mistake stops recurring. Written honestly, it captures the non-obvious lessons (the access that took three weeks, the data assumption that was wrong) that would otherwise be forgotten. For incidents, it's how a failure becomes prevention. And the discipline of honest reflection, owning what went wrong without blame or spin, is a professional maturity customers and teams value. Turning it into a highlight reel wastes the exercise.

## Key concepts

- **Structure:** what broke / why (root cause) / what changed / how to prevent recurrence.
- **Blameless:** systems and decisions, not individuals, so people are honest.
- **Root cause, not symptom:** ask why repeatedly to reach the real cause.
- **Actionable prevention:** specific changes with owners, not "be more careful."
- **Not success marketing:** the honest failures are the point, not a wins list.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Postmortems assign blame so it won't recur."** Blame drives honesty out; blameless analysis prevents recurrence better.
- **"Report the wins."** A postmortem that's success marketing is useless; its value is the honest account of what went wrong.
- **"The lesson is 'be more careful.'"** Useful postmortems produce specific, owned, actionable changes.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>What goes in a postmortem and why keep it blameless?</summary>

What broke, why (root causes, not symptoms), what changed, and how to prevent recurrence, with specific, owned actions. It's blameless, focused on systems, decisions, and process rather than individuals, because blame makes people hide what really happened, and the honest root causes are exactly what you need to prevent recurrence. And it's not success marketing; the failures are the point.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you make postmortem lessons actually stick?</summary>

Make them specific and actionable ("confirm data access in week one," not "plan better"), assign owners, and feed them into how the next engagement runs, checklists, the scoping template, team practices. A lesson that isn't turned into a concrete, owned change is just a note; the value is changing what you do next time.

</details>
