**In one sentence:** As-Is is how the workflow runs today; To-Be is how it will run after your solution; and documenting both, the business-process change, not just the software feature, is what makes the value clear and the change adoptable.

## What it is

**As-Is** captures the current workflow (from the mapping page) with its steps, handoffs, and pain. **To-Be** captures the redesigned workflow after the solution: which steps change, which are automated or AI-assisted, which handoffs disappear, and how work flows differently. The crucial framing: document the **business-process change**, not merely "we added feature X." The feature is a means; the value is the changed process, fewer handoffs, faster cycle time, less manual work. Showing As-Is → To-Be makes both the improvement and the required behavior change explicit.

## Why an FDE needs this

Stakeholders adopt a *process*, not a feature. If you only describe the software, they can't see how their work actually changes, or prepare for it. The As-Is → To-Be contrast is how you communicate value ("here's the 6-step handoff-heavy process today; here's the 3-step one after"), justify the project, and surface the change-management work adoption will require. It ties discovery and mapping to a concrete, agreed picture of the improvement.

## Key concepts

- **As-Is:** the current workflow, warts and all.
- **To-Be:** the redesigned workflow after the solution.
- **Process change over feature change:** describe how work flows differently, not just what was built.
- **Value made visible:** the delta (steps, handoffs, time, effort) is the value.
- **Adoption implications:** To-Be reveals the behavior changes people must make.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Describing the feature is enough."** Stakeholders adopt a process; show how the workflow changes, not just the software.
- **"To-Be is just As-Is with the tool added."** It's a redesigned flow, often with fewer steps and handoffs, not the old process plus a button.
- **"The change is automatic once we ship."** To-Be surfaces the behavior change people must make; adoption needs managing.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>Why document As-Is and To-Be rather than just the feature?</summary>

Because value and adoption live in the process, not the feature. As-Is shows the current workflow and its pain; To-Be shows the redesigned flow after the solution, fewer handoffs, less manual work, faster cycle. The contrast makes the improvement concrete, justifies the project, and reveals the behavior changes adoption will require, which a feature description alone hides.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the risk of only capturing the software change?</summary>

Stakeholders can't see how their daily work actually changes, so they can't evaluate the value or prepare to adopt it. You also miss the change-management work, training, new handoffs, trust, that determines whether the solution gets used. The process view (To-Be) is what makes those visible.

</details>
