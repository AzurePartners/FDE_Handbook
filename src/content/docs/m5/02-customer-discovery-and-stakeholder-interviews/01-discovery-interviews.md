**In one sentence:** A discovery interview systematically uncovers the customer's business goals, current workflow, pain points, constraints, supporting evidence, and who actually decides, so you understand the real problem before proposing a solution.

## What it is

**Discovery** is the deliberate work of understanding before designing. A good discovery interview covers: the **business goal** (what outcome do they want?), the **current workflow** (how is this done today, step by step?), the **pain points** (what specifically hurts, and how much?), **constraints** (technical, budget, timeline, compliance), **evidence** (data or examples that show the problem is real and sized), and the **decision makers** (who can approve, fund, and adopt this?). The aim is a grounded picture of the problem, not a feature list.

## Why an FDE needs this

Customers usually present a *solution* ("build an AI chatbot"), not their problem. Building the stated request when it's a mis-guess is the most common, expensive FDE failure. Discovery is the cheapest place to change direction, a wrong assumption caught in conversation costs a conversation; caught after building, a rebuild. Strong discovery is what customers and interviewers most want to see.

## Key concepts

- **Business goal:** the outcome behind the request.
- **Current workflow:** the concrete as-is process.
- **Pain points:** what hurts, how badly, how often.
- **Constraints:** technical, budget, timeline, compliance.
- **Evidence:** data/examples proving and sizing the problem.
- **Decision makers:** who approves, funds, and adopts.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The customer's request is the requirement."** It's usually a proposed solution; discovery finds the real problem beneath it.
- **"Discovery slows delivery."** It prevents building the wrong thing, which is far slower than a few good conversations.
- **"More requirement docs = better discovery."** Discovery is understanding; a sharp conversation beats a long form.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>A customer says "build us an AI chatbot." How do you run discovery?</summary>

I treat the chatbot as a proposed solution and dig into the problem: what business outcome they want, how the task is handled today and what specifically is painful, what constraints exist, what evidence shows the problem's size, and who decides and would adopt it. Often the real need points to a simpler or different solution than a chatbot.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What should you leave a discovery interview with?</summary>

A grounded understanding of the business goal, the current workflow, the specific and sized pain points, the constraints, the evidence the problem is real, and who the decision makers are, not a feature list. That's the basis for scoping the right thing.

</details>
