---
title: Feeding Learnings Back to Product
row: M5-L7.8
---
**In one sentence:** Field learnings from an engagement, what customers really need, where the product fell short, what had to be built around it, should flow back to the product team in a prioritized, actionable way, so the product improves and each subsequent deployment gets cheaper.

## What it is

An FDE sits at the frontier where the product meets real customers, and that vantage produces valuable **field learnings**: recurring needs the product doesn't meet, rough edges customers hit, workarounds you had to build, features that would have saved days. **Feeding these back** to the product team means reporting them in a form the team can act on, concrete, prioritized by frequency and impact, with context, not just a vent. Done well, the product absorbs common needs (so FDEs stop rebuilding them), and the **second deployment gets cheaper** because the product now does what previously required custom work. It closes the lifecycle loop (Product Feedback in M5-L1.2).

## Why an FDE needs this

Without this loop, every FDE keeps solving the same product gaps by hand, expensive and non-compounding. With it, the FDE function makes the product better, which is leverage: a gap fixed in the product helps every future engagement. But the feedback has to be useful, prioritized, concrete, evidenced, or it's ignored. An FDE who reports field learnings well shapes the product and reduces their own future workload; one who doesn't leaves the org rebuilding the same workarounds forever.

## Key concepts

- **FDE as product sensor:** the frontier view surfaces real, recurring needs and gaps.
- **What to report:** unmet needs, rough edges, workarounds built, high-value missing features.
- **Prioritize:** rank by frequency and impact so the team can act.
- **Actionable & evidenced:** concrete, with context, not a vague complaint.
- **Cheaper next time:** product absorbing common needs is what compounds across deployments.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Delivery ends at handoff."** The lifecycle includes feeding learnings back; skipping it forfeits the compounding benefit.
- **"Just send the team a list of complaints."** Feedback must be prioritized, concrete, and evidenced to be acted on.
- **"Product feedback isn't the FDE's job."** The FDE's frontier position is exactly where the most valuable product signal comes from.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>Why and how should an FDE feed learnings back to the product team?</summary>

Because the FDE sees where the product meets real customers, and recurring gaps and workarounds are valuable signal. I report them concretely and prioritized by frequency and impact, with context and evidence, not as a vent. When the product absorbs common needs, FDEs stop rebuilding them and the next similar deployment gets cheaper, which is real leverage.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How does feeding learnings back make the second deployment cheaper?</summary>

The first engagement reveals what the product lacks and what had to be built around it. If those recurring gaps are reported well and the product team fixes them, the next similar deployment gets that capability out of the box instead of as custom work, so it's faster and cheaper. It turns one-off delivery effort into a durable product improvement.

</details>
