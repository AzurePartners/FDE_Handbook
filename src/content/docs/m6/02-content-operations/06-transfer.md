**In one sentence:** The Marketing Studio pattern of intake, writing from canonical facts, independent review, templated design and human sign-off carries over to any business that produces a steady stream of rule-bound content.

## What stays the same

Take away the course posters and five structural choices remain. They are what you reuse:

1. **One front-door agent** that clarifies the request and owns the conversation, so specialists never ask the user the same question twice.
2. **Canonical facts in files**, one place per fact, which the writer must cite and the reviewer checks against.
3. **An independent reviewer** that reads the finished output, not the creator's summary, and can block it.
4. **Templates with locked layout**, so brand consistency is enforced by structure rather than by instruction.
5. **A human at the end**, who edits, confirms and publishes.

## What changes by industry

| Industry | Typical request | Canonical facts | What the reviewer must catch | Human gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Education | Course poster, open-day page | Courses, dates, prices, faculty | Wrong price or date, unapproved outcome claims | Marketing lead |
| SaaS | Feature launch page, release email | Feature list, plan limits, pricing | Features not yet shipped, wrong plan limits | Product marketing and product owner |
| Healthcare marketing | Clinic service page, patient newsletter | Services offered, clinician credentials | Medical claims that need substantiation or approval | Compliance or medical reviewer |
| E-commerce | Product listing, promotion banner | Catalogue, stock, promotion terms | Expired promotions, prices that differ from the catalogue | Merchandiser |
| Social-media operations | Post series across channels | Brand voice guide, campaign calendar | Channel rule violations, off-brand tone, missing disclosures | Social lead |

The reviewer's checklist is where the industries differ most. In healthcare marketing it becomes the heaviest part of the system, and the human gate may need a named approver with sign-off recorded. In e-commerce the risk shifts to freshness: a promotion that ended yesterday is a wrong fact.

## Discussion exercise

Pick two industries from the table. For each, write down:

- the files that would hold the canonical facts, and who owns them;
- the three most expensive mistakes the reviewer must block;
- whether publishing can ever be automatic, and what would have to be true first.

Then compare your two answers. The agent roles should be almost identical. The review rules and the human gate should not be.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Reuse means copying the prompts."** What transfers is the structure. The facts, the review rules and the approval path must be rebuilt for each client.
- **"A stricter industry needs more agents."** It usually needs a stricter reviewer checklist and a named human approver, not more workers.
