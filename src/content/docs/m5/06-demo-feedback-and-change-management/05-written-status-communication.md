**In one sentence:** Regular written status updates, weekly progress and clear risk flags, keep remote stakeholders aligned without meetings, and doing them well (concise, honest, leading with risks and asks) is a quiet superpower in customer-facing work.

## What it is

A **status update** tells stakeholders where things stand: what got done, what's next, what's blocked or at risk, and any decisions or access needed from them. Delivered on a regular **cadence** (weekly is common) and **in writing** so it's asynchronous and referenceable. A good update is short, **leads with what matters** (risks, blockers, asks, not a task diary), is **honest** about problems, and makes any needed decision easy to act on. **Risk flags**, raising a slip or concern early, are the most valuable part.

## Why an FDE needs this

FDE work is often remote and cross-time-zone (this program itself runs TA support on WeChat within set hours), so async written communication is how alignment is maintained between demos and meetings. A crisp weekly update surfaces risks early, creates a record, and signals professionalism and control. Poor communication, silence, or rambling updates that bury the important part, erodes confidence even when the work is fine. Clear written updates are what earn the trust to work independently.

## Key concepts

- **Cadence:** regular and predictable (e.g. weekly).
- **Lead with risks & asks:** blockers and needed decisions first, not a chronological log.
- **Honest & specific:** name real risks and slips; vague "all good" loses credibility.
- **Actionable asks:** state needed decisions/access with owners and deadlines.
- **Written & async:** referenceable, respects time zones, cuts meetings.

```
Weekly update:
- Done: ...
- Next: ...
- Risks / blockers: ...
- Need from you (decision / access, by when): ...
```

## Common misconceptions

- **"No news is fine if work's progressing."** Silence reads as trouble and hides risk; update on cadence regardless.
- **"An update is a full activity log."** Burying the important part defeats the purpose; lead with risks and asks.
- **"Only report good news."** Honest risk flags are what make updates trustworthy and let problems be handled early.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>What makes a good written status update?</summary>

It's regular, short, and leads with what matters, risks, blockers, and any decisions or access needed from the stakeholder, rather than a chronological task log. It's honest about problems so it stays credible, and it makes asks actionable with owners and deadlines. Written and async so it's referenceable and works across time zones.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why communicate status in writing on a cadence rather than only in meetings?</summary>

Because async written updates keep everyone aligned without meeting overhead, work across time zones, create a durable record, and surface risks early on a predictable rhythm. Silence between meetings breeds anxiety and hides problems; a reliable weekly update signals control and earns the trust to work independently.

</details>
