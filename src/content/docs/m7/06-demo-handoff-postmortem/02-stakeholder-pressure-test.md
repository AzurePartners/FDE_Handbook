**In one sentence:** The stakeholder pressure test is a live Q and A where a reviewer plays the client and pushes with new requests and objections, and you answer with time, cost, and risk instead of agreeing to everything.

## What it is

After the demo, the client reacts. A coordinator says, "Can it also grade assignments by Friday?" The operations lead says, "Why not just use the free chatbot?" A reviewer plays these roles and you defend your decisions.

The skill is not saying no. It is turning a request into a decision the client can make with facts. How to negotiate and decline without losing trust is covered in [Negotiating Trade-offs & Saying No Without Losing Trust](../../m5/06-demo-feedback-and-change-management/03-negotiating-trade-offs-and-saying-no-without-losing-trust.md), and sorting requests into clarification, change, or future phase in [Classifying New Requests](../../m5/04-scope-prd-sow-acceptance-criteria-and-non-goals/05-classifying-new-requests-clarification-change-or-future.md) (M5). This page is the rehearsal you submit.

## Why an FDE needs this

Agreeing to everything feels friendly and then wrecks the schedule. Refusing flatly feels firm and then wrecks the relationship. Clients also test whether you understand your own system; vague answers cost credibility.

## Key concepts

### What you produce

A pressure-test sheet: six or more prompts, your prepared answer to each, and the classification behind it.

| Prompt from the client | Class | Answer pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "Can it also grade assignments by Friday?" | Future phase | Grading is a different risk class and needs a rubric and review process. By Friday we can deliver X. Grading would need about Y time and a human sign-off. Your call on priority. |
| "Why not just use a general chatbot?" | Objection | It has no access to your current policies and gives no citations. Show the changed-deadline case. |
| "It got one answer wrong. Is it broken?" | Objection | Point to eval results and the limitation list, and show the fallback to a support agent. |
| "Can students ask in any language?" | Future phase | It is a non-goal for this delivery and untested. Here is what testing it requires in the next phase. |
| "Can we skip the review step to save time?" | Trade-off | Cost saved versus risk of wrong refund answers. Offer a limited version. |

New requests take one of the three M5 classes; objections and trade-offs are labeled as such. Answer shape: restate the need, classify it, give time, cost, and risk, offer two options, ask for the decision, and record it in the change log.

### Pass bar

- Every prepared answer names an impact on time, cost, or risk, not only "yes" or "no."
- At least one request is declined or deferred with a reason and an alternative.
- No unscoped commitment is made in the room. Anything accepted is logged.
- Questions about accuracy are answered with your own evaluation results, not general claims.
- A reviewer playing the client says they know what happens next.

## Common misconceptions

- **"A good FDE says yes to the customer."** A good FDE says what each yes costs and lets the customer choose.
- **"Saying no damages the relationship."** Unexplained refusals do. A no with an alternative and a path forward usually builds trust.
- **"Pressure questions are hostile."** They are the questions the real client will ask later. Hear them from a reviewer first, and answer with evidence, not by attacking the question.
- **"A quick estimate is better than none."** An estimate with no reasoning becomes a commitment. Give the reasoning, and write down what was agreed.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>A stakeholder asks for a new feature during your demo. What do you say?</summary>

Acknowledge the need, ask what problem it solves, and say it is outside the current scope. Give a rough impact on time, cost, and risk, offer options, and promise a written decision by a date. Do not commit in the room.

</details>

<details>
<summary>The client says "just make it work for everything." How do you respond?</summary>

Ask for the three highest-value cases. Show what is covered and tested, and propose a phased plan so each addition comes with its own evaluation.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What would you do differently under pressure next time?</summary>

Name a real habit, such as pausing to classify the request before answering.

</details>
