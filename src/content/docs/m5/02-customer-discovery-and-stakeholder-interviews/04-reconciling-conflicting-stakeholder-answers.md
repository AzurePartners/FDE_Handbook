title: Reconciling Conflicting Stakeholder Answers
row: M5-L2.4

**In one sentence:** Business, IT, operations, and finance will describe the same problem differently, and reconciling those conflicting answers, understanding why each sees it their way and finding the shared truth, is a core discovery skill, not a sign someone is wrong.

## What it is

Ask four departments about the same process and you'll get four different problems. **Business** frames it as revenue or customer outcomes; **IT** as systems and constraints; **operations** as daily workflow pain; **finance** as cost and risk. These aren't contradictions so much as different vantage points, each true from where they sit. **Reconciling** them means recognizing the pattern, probing why each holds their view, and synthesizing a problem definition that accounts for all of them, or surfacing a genuine conflict (e.g. business wants speed, security wants control) that has to be decided rather than papered over.

## Why an FDE needs this

If you take one department's version as the whole truth, you'll design something that fails the others, fast but insecure, or compliant but unusable. Conflicting answers are normal and informative: the differences reveal the full shape of the problem and the real trade-offs. An FDE has to hold multiple perspectives, find the shared underlying problem, and escalate true conflicts to the decision maker instead of silently picking a side.

## Key concepts

- **Different lenses, not lies:** each function sees a real facet (revenue / systems / workflow / cost).
- **Probe the "why":** understand what drives each view.
- **Synthesize:** find the shared underlying problem across perspectives.
- **Surface genuine conflicts:** where views truly clash (speed vs control), name it for a decision.
- **Don't silently pick a side:** unresolved conflicts resurface later, worse.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Conflicting answers mean someone's wrong."** Usually each is right from their vantage; the differences map the real problem.
- **"Pick the most senior person's version."** Seniority isn't accuracy; synthesize, and escalate true trade-offs explicitly.
- **"Smooth over the disagreement."** Papered-over conflicts resurface during build or launch; resolve them up front.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>Business, IT, and operations describe the same problem differently. How do you handle it?</summary>

I treat the differences as different vantage points, each true from where they sit, and probe why each holds their view. Then I synthesize a problem definition that accounts for all of them. Where views genuinely conflict, like business wanting speed and security wanting control, I surface it explicitly for the decision maker rather than silently choosing, since an unresolved conflict resurfaces later.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why are conflicting stakeholder answers actually useful?</summary>

Because together they reveal the full shape of the problem and the real trade-offs, which a single perspective would hide. Reconciling them gives a more complete, accurate problem definition and flags the tensions that the solution and the stakeholders will have to decide on deliberately.

</details>
