**In one sentence:** Feedback is only useful if it's closed into a loop, captured as an issue, prioritized, decided on, and recorded in a change log, so nothing is lost, decisions are traceable, and the customer sees their input acted on.

## What it is

Demos and use generate feedback; a **closed loop** turns it into action and record. The steps: **feedback** comes in; it's captured as a discrete **issue** (not left as a vague comment); it's **prioritized** against everything else; a **decision** is made (do now / later / won't do, with reasoning); and it's recorded in a **change log** that tracks what changed, when, and why. The loop ensures feedback isn't forgotten, prioritization is deliberate, decisions are visible, and there's a durable trail of how the system and scope evolved.

## Why an FDE needs this

Uncaptured feedback gets lost, and undecided feedback festers ("did they ever do what I asked?"). Closing the loop shows the customer their input is taken seriously and acted on (even a "not now, because…" is respectful and clear), keeps prioritization intentional rather than loudest-voice-wins, and produces a change log that prevents disputes and supports handoff. It's the operational backbone of change management and the trust it builds is significant.

## Key concepts

- **Capture as an issue:** turn vague feedback into a concrete, trackable item.
- **Prioritize:** weigh against everything else, not first-come or loudest.
- **Decide explicitly:** now / later / won't-do, with reasoning.
- **Change log:** record what changed, when, and why, a durable, traceable trail.
- **Visible to the customer:** they see their input acknowledged and its disposition.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Noted feedback is handled feedback."** Until it's an issue with a decision, it's likely lost; capture and decide explicitly.
- **"Only 'yes' decisions need recording."** A recorded "not now, because…" is respectful, clear, and prevents the question resurfacing.
- **"A change log is bureaucracy."** It's what prevents disputes, supports handoff, and shows the customer their input mattered.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>How do you handle feedback from a demo or from users?</summary>

I close the loop: capture each piece as a concrete issue, prioritize it against everything else, make an explicit decision (do now, later, or won't-do with reasoning), and record it in a change log with what changed, when, and why. That ensures nothing is lost, prioritization is deliberate, decisions are visible to the customer, and there's a traceable trail.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why record decisions to not do something?</summary>

Because unrecorded "no"s resurface as "did you ever handle my request?" and erode trust. A recorded "not now, because…" with reasoning is respectful and clear, shows the input was taken seriously, and prevents the same request from being re-litigated, while keeping the change log an accurate history of decisions.

</details>
