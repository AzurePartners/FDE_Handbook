title: "Classifying New Requests: Clarification, Change or Future Phase"
row: M5-L4.5

**In one sentence:** When a new request arrives mid-project, classify it, a clarification of existing scope, a formal change to scope, or future-phase work, and record it accordingly, so scope stays controlled and nothing is silently absorbed or lost.

## What it is

Requests during a build come in three kinds. A **clarification** refines something already in scope (no scope change, just detail). A **formal change** adds or alters scope and must go through the change mechanism (impact on timeline/cost assessed and agreed, per the SOW). **Future-phase** work is valid but out of this scope, recorded on a roadmap for later. Classifying each request, and recording it in the right place, is how you handle change without either rigidly refusing everything or silently absorbing additions until scope explodes.

## Why an FDE needs this

Uncontrolled absorption of "just one more thing" is how time-boxed engagements blow their timelines; flat refusal makes you unresponsive. Classifying gives a disciplined middle path: clarifications you just handle, changes go through an agreed process with visible trade-offs, future work is captured so "not now" isn't "never." It keeps scope controlled while staying responsive, and creates a record that prevents disputes about what was agreed.

## Key concepts

- **Clarification:** detail on in-scope work; no scope change.
- **Formal change:** alters scope; goes through the change mechanism with impact assessed.
- **Future-phase:** valid but out of scope now; roadmap it.
- **Record each:** log the request and its classification; don't decide by memory.
- **Middle path:** neither absorb-everything nor refuse-everything.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Every request is a change."** Some are just clarifications of existing scope; over-formalizing those adds friction.
- **"Just absorb small additions."** Unclassified absorption is exactly how scope creeps; even small changes get classified.
- **"Saying it's future-phase is a brush-off."** Recorded on a real roadmap, it's a commitment to revisit, not a no.

## Typical interview questions

<details>
<summary>A stakeholder asks for something new mid-build. How do you handle it?</summary>

I classify it: a clarification of existing scope (just handle it), a formal change (run it through the change mechanism, assess and agree the timeline/cost impact), or future-phase work (record it on the roadmap for later). I log the request and its classification so scope stays controlled and there's no dispute later about what was agreed.

</details>

<summary>Why classify rather than just accept or reject requests?</summary>
