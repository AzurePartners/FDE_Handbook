**In one sentence:** An FDE's skills span eight dimensions, Systems, AI, Agents, Integration, Production, Discovery, Communication, and Domain, and different FDE roles weight them differently, so knowing the model helps you see what "good" looks like and where to grow.

## What it is

The role is broad, and the **capability model** breaks it into eight dimensions:

- **Systems:** software/systems fundamentals (Module 1).
- **AI:** LLM application engineering (Module 2).
- **Agents:** agentic patterns and orchestration (Module 3).
- **Integration:** connecting to enterprise systems, auth, data (Module 4).
- **Production:** reliability, deployment, observability (Module 4).
- **Discovery:** finding the real problem, requirements (Module 5).
- **Communication:** stakeholders, demos, writing (Module 5).
- **Domain:** knowledge of the customer's field (finance, healthcare).

For each, there's a sense of what "good" looks like, and different FDE roles emphasize different dimensions (a data-heavy engagement weights Integration/Production; a greenfield advisory one weights Discovery/Communication).

## Why an FDE needs this

The model is a map of the role and a self-development tool: it shows the full scope (so you don't mistake strong coding for being a strong FDE), lets you assess your own strengths and gaps honestly, and helps you understand what a given engagement or role will demand most. It's also the through-line of this whole handbook, each module builds specific dimensions.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Being a strong coder makes you a strong FDE."** Coding is one or two dimensions; Discovery, Communication, and Domain matter as much.
- **"Every FDE needs all eight maxed."** Roles weight them differently; you develop toward the mix your work needs.
- **"Domain knowledge is optional."** In finance/healthcare it's often decisive; it's an explicit dimension.

<summary>What are the dimensions of the FDE capability model?</summary>

Systems, AI, Agents, Integration, Production, Discovery, Communication, and Domain. The first five are technical (fundamentals through production integration), the last three are the consulting and context skills, finding the real problem, communicating with stakeholders, and knowing the customer's field. Different FDE roles weight them differently.

</details>

Because it maps the full role, so I don't mistake strong coding for being a strong FDE. It lets me assess my strengths and gaps across all eight dimensions honestly and grow toward the mix a given engagement demands, for example investing in Domain and Discovery for a new industry, not just more Systems depth.

</details>
